## 1. Statutory Notice Shortfall & Basic vs. Gross Buyout Formula
* **File:** [NoticeAdjustmentCalculatorPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/NoticeAdjustmentCalculatorPage.jsx) / [NoticeBuyoutCalculatorPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/NoticeBuyoutCalculatorPage.jsx)
* **Claim:** Notice period shortfall recovery should be calculated on **Basic Salary + DA** rather than **Gross CTC** unless the employment agreement explicitly defines buyout on gross salary.
* **Current Source:** State Shops & Commercial Establishments Acts (e.g. Karnataka S&E Act Sec 39, TN S&E Act Sec 41).
* **Reason Verification Is Required:** Employers frequently attempt to recover gross salary for unserved notice days. Judicial precedents vary across High Courts depending on whether the clause is construed as liquidated damages (Sec 74 Contract Act) or standard statutory notice pay.
* **Recommended Verification Source:** High Court judgements under Section 74 of Indian Contract Act 1872 & State Shops Acts.
* **Risk:** Medium — Potential contractual disputes between employer contract terms and statutory defaults.

## 2. 4 Years & 240 Days Gratuity Eligibility Rule
* **File:** [GratuityCalculatorPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/GratuityCalculatorPage.jsx) / [GratuityPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/GratuityPage.jsx)
* **Claim:** An employee completing 4 years and 240 days (or 190 days in 5-day workweek establishments) of continuous service qualifies for statutory gratuity payout under Section 2A of the Payment of Gratuity Act, 1972.
* **Current Source:** Supreme Court of India in *Madras Fertilizers Ltd. v. Controlling Authority* & High Court precedents.
* **Reason Verification Is Required:** While Controlling Authorities (Labour Commissioners) routinely award gratuity for 4 years 240 days, some corporate HR teams strictly insist on 5 full calendar years (1825 days).
* **Recommended Verification Source:** Controlling Authority & Appellate Authority rulings under Payment of Gratuity Act 1972.
* **Risk:** Low-Medium — Educational guidance is clearly tagged with Supreme Court citations.

## 3. Employer EPF 12% Contribution Shifted to Employee Gross Salary
* **File:** [CTCDeductionScannerPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/CTCDeductionScannerPage.jsx) / [PFAnalyzerPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/PFAnalyzerPage.jsx)
* **Claim:** Statutory employer EPF contribution (12%) is an employer liability under Section 6 of EPF Act 1952 and cannot be subtracted from an employee's monthly gross take-home wages.
* **Current Source:** Employees' Provident Funds & Misc. Provisions Act 1952 / *Surya Roshni Ltd. v. EPFO* & *Vivekananda Vidyamandir* (SC 2019).
* **Reason Verification Is Required:** CTC agreements in private startups frequently structure Total Cost to Company including employer PF. Differentiating between "Total CTC" and "Gross Monthly Take-Home Wages" requires checking specific employment appointment terms.
* **Recommended Verification Source:** EPFO Circulars & High Court Labour Division Rulings.
* **Risk:** Low — Tool uses cautious language ("Potential issue identified", "Requires verification").

## 4. Post-Employment Non-Compete Invalidation Under Section 27
* **File:** [ClauseAnalyzerPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/ClauseAnalyzerPage.jsx) / [EmploymentBondScannerPage.jsx](file:///home/thanish/.gemini/antigravity/scratch/employee-rights-india/src/pages/EmploymentBondScannerPage.jsx)
* **Claim:** Post-employment non-compete restrictions restricting an employee from joining a competitor after resignation are void and unenforceable under Section 27 of the Indian Contract Act, 1872.
* **Current Source:** Supreme Court of India in *Percept D'Mark (India) Pvt. Ltd. v. Zaheer Khan* (2006) 4 SCC 227 & *Niranjan Shankar Golikari v. Century Spg. & Mfg. Co. Ltd.* (1967).
* **Reason Verification Is Required:** While post-employment non-competes are void, non-disclosure (confidentiality) and non-solicitation of clients/employees during or post-employment remain enforceable if reasonable.
* **Recommended Verification Source:** Supreme Court of India Contract Division Rulings.
* **Risk:** Low — Cites landmark Supreme Court cases directly.
